## What only HR can decide

In the order they block things. The first two gate everything downstream.

### 1. Ratify the approval bar — *blocks everything*
Every quality rule the system enforces is currently a provisional setting we chose. The register
holds **197 entries**, but only about **60 are genuine policy** — the rest are technical tuning
that should never need HR. **None has been ratified.**

**Ask:** a working session on the ~60 policy items, grouped into a dozen packages. Until they are
signed, the system enforces our guesses *consistently* — useful, but not SFU policy.

### 2. Run the pilot: 5–10 roles, one real reviewer — *blocks everything*
Four JDs have been approved. Nothing about the review experience has been tested by someone who
does this work for a living.

**Ask:** name a reviewer and a date.

### 3. CUPE — in or out? — *scope*
The Bank serves **37%** of the archive today (APSA / APEX / Polytechnic). CUPE is **31%** and is
deliberately excluded: it uses a different instrument (the WJQ) with no agreed quality bar, so
scoring it against the JDFN template would produce confidently wrong answers.

**Ask:** should CUPE be in scope? If yes, that is a separate project — HR defines a CUPE quality
bar first, then we build to it.

*The remaining **32%** is our problem, not theirs: we cannot yet reliably tell which employee
group those files belong to. Say so plainly; it is an engineering gap we are fixing.*

### 4. The pay-grade scales, per employee group — *blocks grades*
We can read CUPE grades out of the documents. APSA and APEX grades mostly live in the HRIS, not
the JD, so the system has nothing to check them against.

**Ask:** the valid grade values for each group. A fifteen-minute answer that unblocks a feature.

### 5. HRIS export & a privacy review — *blocks import*
Pulling grades from the HRIS means moving compensation data. That needs a FIPPA look before we
write a line of it.

**Ask:** who owns the export, and who signs the privacy review.

### 6. Sign off the standard footer — *blocks publishing*
Every generated JD carries SFU boilerplate including the territorial acknowledgement. We must not
ship our own wording of it.

**Ask:** confirm the current official text.

### Already fixed — nothing to decide
Three rules were mis-scoring the archive. They were **our defects**, not judgement calls —
corrected, and the whole archive re-scored. **The numbers in the review packet are the corrected
ones.**

## If someone asks how good the JDs are

Measured over the **874** job descriptions that represent current practice — not the whole
archive, which mixes in decades of superseded templates.

| Measure | Result | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Would pass the bar today | **78.6%** | Roughly four in five need no structural change |
| Median quality score | **79.05** | Out of 100, against the published standard |
| Grade A / B | 81 / 551 | The bulk sit at B — sound, with small fixes |
| Grade C / D | 240 / 2 | C needs real work; D is vanishingly rare |

The single most common blocker is a position summary over 150 words — not a deep structural
problem.

## If something goes wrong

- **A page asks you to sign in.** That is CAS doing its job — sign in with your SFU credentials.
- **A form says "Forbidden".** The tab has been open too long. CSRF protection landed this
  morning; reload the page and redo the action.
- **A role's title looks like a paragraph.** Known: about 4% of titles are still a sentence lifted
  from the document. Move on — in particular do not open the published role titled *"In
  collaboration with the Communications Officer"*.
- **The Builder's duplicate panel doesn't appear.** It needs a few sentences of content first, and
  it needs the inference host reachable. Skip it rather than waiting.
- **Anything else.** `http://localhost:25800/ready` names the unhappy store in one line.
