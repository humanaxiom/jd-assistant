# JD Bank — status and re-evaluation, 2026-08-24

**Written because the project felt like a loop. It is one, and this document is the
evidence, the diagnosis and the way out.** Every number here is a query against the live
database or the repo, reproduced inline so it can be checked rather than believed.

---

## 1. The one-paragraph answer

The engineering is in good shape and is not the problem. **14,565 job descriptions are
ingested, parsed, deduplicated, clustered, harmonized and scored; 2,489 canonical drafts
exist; 1,292 of them are approvable right now.** The system has a UI, SSO, a searchable
library, a builder and a review queue, behind 2,890 tests at 93% coverage. What it does
not have is **output**: five JDs have ever been published, and eight human review actions
have ever been taken. **The binding constraint has been human review and HR ratification
since roughly 2026-07-21, and every week since has been spent on engineering we could do
alone instead of on the decision we cannot.**

## 4. Why it is a loop, stated plainly

The work has followed one cycle, repeatedly:

```
measure the archive → find a content defect → register a decision → fix it
    → re-run the producer (11–19 GPU-hours) → measure again → find the next defect
```

Every iteration was individually correct. The defects were real — fabricated duties,
truncated point-factor blocks, emptied relationship sections, a parser blind to 16% of
CUPE documents. Each fix is defensible on its own.

**But the cycle has no exit condition.** At 14,522 documents there is always another
defect, and "carry-through is not yet 100%" will always be true of something. The loop
optimises a *proxy* (draft fidelity) and never touches the *goal* (approved JDs), so it
can run indefinitely while the goal stays at five.

**The plan itself named the exit a month ago and it was not taken.** `docs/plan.md:511`,
written for Phase 4.6 which shipped **2026-07-21**:

> **4.5 (the human pilot) is now the next milestone.**

Since that line was written: Phase 5, Phase 8, Phase 9, Phases A–G, the CUPE cohort, two
re-baselines, a parser version bump and this week's two content-loss fixes. **The pilot
has still not happened.**

## 5. The most expensive fact: quality was never the CUPE blocker

This is the finding that should change what we do next.

```sql
-- drafts blocked, by gate, per form
```

| form | gate | drafts blocked |
|---|---|---:|
| **wjq (CUPE)** | `SFU-APPROVE-QUAL-EQUIVALENT` | **620 of 649 (95.5%)** |
| **wjq (CUPE)** | `SFU-APPROVE-KSA-ORDER` | **564 of 649 (86.9%)** |
| wjq (CUPE) | `SFU-APPROVE-NO-PLACEHOLDERS` | 111 |
| jdfn | `SFU-APPROVE-KSA-ORDER` | 323 |
| jdfn | `SFU-APPROVE-QUAL-EQUIVALENT` | 145 |

CUPE approvable: **3 of 649 (0.5%)**.

**Both top CUPE blockers are registered, unratified HR policy decisions** — not content
defects, not parser gaps, not anything the rewrite touches. `SFU-APPROVE-QUAL-EQUIVALENT`
alone blocks **95.5% of the CUPE cohort**, and it blocks them for a wording requirement
("an equivalent combination of education and experience") that a 1990s Weighted Job
Questionnaire had no reason to contain.

**We just spent 19.4 GPU-hours and a week of engineering making CUPE drafts better, and
the approvable count went 6 → 3.** That was the *correct* outcome of the fixes — HR-213
means a draft with no real duties now fails honestly instead of passing on invention —
but it makes the point sharply: **content quality was never the binding constraint on the
CUPE cohort, and improving it cannot move the number that matters.**

## 7. Why this happened — and why "we shipped two systems on Codex" is a fair comparison

The honest read is not that the engineering was slow. 223 commits, 2,890 tests and a
working two-form pipeline in six weeks is not slow.

**It is that this project's definition of done depends on a decision someone else has to
make, and that decision was never scheduled.** When the critical path runs through an
external human and nobody books the meeting, work flows to whatever *can* be done
unilaterally — and there is always more measuring, more fidelity, more defects to find.
The two systems delivered on Codex almost certainly had acceptance criteria the team
controlled end-to-end. That is the difference. It is a project-management failure, not a
tooling one, and not a competence one.

**I contributed to it directly.** This week I found two genuine content-loss defects,
fixed them properly, measured them honestly — and never once asked whether draft content
was the thing standing between this project and a published JD. It was not. Fifteen
minutes of the SQL in §5 would have said so at any point in the last month.

## 8. What changes

### STOP
- **No further producer/parser/rewrite quality work** until there is output. The
  duty-frequency defect (27.7% vs 92.3%) is real, registered and **can wait** — it
  affects a CUPE point-factor evaluation on a cohort that is 99.5% blocked for unrelated
  reasons.
- **No more full-archive producer runs.** Each costs ~19 GPU-hours and moves the
  deliverable by zero.

### START — in this order
1. **The JDFN pilot, this week.** Put ~20 of the 1,292 approvable drafts in front of a
   real HR reviewer and have them approve, reject or edit. **Success = 20 published JDs
   and a list of what the reviewer actually complained about.** That list is worth more
   than any measurement we can take ourselves.
2. **Get the two gates ruled on.** `SFU-APPROVE-QUAL-EQUIVALENT` and
   `SFU-APPROVE-KSA-ORDER` gate 620 and 564 CUPE drafts respectively. A single HR ruling
   on those two could move CUPE approvable from 3 to several hundred — more than every
   engineering change made this month, combined, at zero GPU cost.
3. **Book the HR ratification session.** 214 decisions, 0 ratified. The matrix
   (`HR-DECISION-MATRIX.md`) has been HR-ready since 2026-08-21. It needs a calendar
   invitation, not another revision.

### CHANGE how done is measured
Replace fidelity proxies with the only number that reflects the goal:

> **Published JDs in the Bank.** Today: 5. Next milestone: 20. Then 100.

`make bank-audit` stays — it is genuinely good and it caught two real defects — but it
measures *draft fidelity*, not *delivery*, and it must stop being read as progress.

## 10. The one open engineering risk worth naming

🔴 **TLS at the edge.** `sfuai.ca:7000` is a Telus NAT forward to plain HTTP on the
public internet, carrying CAS sign-in cookies. If the pilot puts a real SFU HR reviewer
on that host, this stops being a backlog item. **Fix before the pilot, not after.**
