PART 2: SFU JD TEMPLATE — COMPLETE STRUCTURE
=======================================================================

Every SFU JD must contain these sections in order:
A. ABOUT SIMON FRASER UNIVERSITY
B. IDENTIFICATION SECTION
C. POSITION SUMMARY
D. DUTIES AND RESPONSIBILITIES
E. DECISION MAKING / IMPACT OF DECISION MAKING
F. PROBLEM SOLVING AND LEVEL OF SUPERVISION
G. RELATIONSHIPS
H. QUALIFICATIONS
I. TERRITORIAL ACKNOWLEDGEMENT & EDI COMMITMENT (mandatory footer)
J. ADDITIONAL CONTEXTUAL INFORMATION (optional — delete if not used)

--- SECTION A: IDENTIFICATION ---
Fields: Position Title | Department/Unit | Supervisor's Title (title not name) | Position # | Employee Group (APSA/APEX/Poly) | Initial Effective Date | Last Revised Date

--- SECTION B: POSITION SUMMARY ---
Rules:
- One paragraph, 100-150 words MAXIMUM
- Clearly outlines PURPOSE of the role
- Highlights core functions / primary duties
- Describes what role spends most time doing

MUST NOT include:
- Working conditions (hours, on-call, housing, parking)
- Specific job titles of supervisor/peers (use "supervisor")
- Qualifications or competencies ("excellent communicator" etc.)
- Task-level detail
- Departmental information
- Incumbent-specific information

3 Quality Components:
1. OVERVIEW OF ROLE'S PURPOSE: What the role is responsible for
2. SUMMARY OF RESPONSIBILITIES: What leadership/coordination it provides
3. DESCRIPTION OF LEVEL OF WORK: How the role manages/leads/coordinates

CHECKLIST:
[] One paragraph in length
[] Overall purpose clearly stated
[] Overall parameter stated
[] Overall end result stated
[] High-level summary — gives reader quick general understanding
[] Acronyms spelled out

COMMON ERRORS:
x What the job does is not clear
x Too lengthy
x Focuses on what the department accomplishes, not the role
x Focuses on incumbent instead of position duties

WORKED EXAMPLE — Manager, Special Projects:
"The Manager, Special Projects is a key resource, responsible for the identification, design and implementation of a designated portfolio of high-priority academic and co-curricular projects in support of the overall goals and objectives of the unit. The Manager provides leadership and support to advance on organization-wide projects, ensuring the achievement of quality standards, timelines, deliverables and risk mitigation. The role manages all phases of the project life cycle, from project planning, development to implementation, leading the delivery of regular project updates and communications."

Annotation:
1. OVERVIEW OF ROLE'S PURPOSE: "responsible for the identification, design and implementation of..."
2. SUMMARY OF RESPONSIBILITIES: "ensuring the achievement of quality standards, timelines, deliverables and risk mitigation"
3. DESCRIPTION OF LEVEL OF WORK: "manages all phases of the project life cycle"

--- SECTION C: DUTIES AND RESPONSIBILITIES ---
Structure:
- 3-5 Primary Functions ordered by significance (most important first)
- Each function: header + % time in brackets e.g. (60%)
- Core duty statement followed by bulleted responsibilities
- All bullets begin with active verb
- % allocations MUST total 100%
- Indicate activity level: assists / coordinates / manages

What/Why/How structure:
- What: "Processes research grant applications"
- Why: "to ensure policies and guidelines have been met"
- How: "by confirming revisions, checking budget, drawing attention to issues"

Primary Functions: Considerations
- Identify: What the role is doing and responsible for daily
- Describe: How the role performs and executes the responsibility

CHECKLIST:
[] Outlined in order of significance
[] Responsibilities are specific and unique
[] Activities described beyond a 'task' level
[] Appropriate activity levels: assists, coordinates, manages
[] Value-added, unique contributions indicated
[] Concise, active language beginning with a verb

COMMON ERRORS:
x Duplication / unclear / disconnected from purpose
x No action verbs to start duty statements
x More than 3-5 main responsibilities
x Duties that may be required in the future included
x Duties written based on incumbent, not position
x Overlap of duties with supervisor

Primary Functions Examples — Manager, Special Projects:
PRIMARY FUNCTION: Manages a portfolio of academic projects
DESCRIPTION:
- Monitoring timelines and providing updates to senior administrators on achievement of project goals and objectives.
- Conducting cost-benefit analysis and research to develop solutions and make recommendations in support of decision-making.
- Defining, developing and implementing a project management framework, tools, templates, standards and requirements.

PRIMARY FUNCTION: Prioritizes and identifies current and future projects
DESCRIPTION:
- Managing a strategic roadmap to highlight, track and identify projects within a designated portfolio of academic and co-curricular projects.
- Conducting research, defining and quantifying issues and preparing reports.

--- SECTION D: DECISION MAKING ---
Purpose: Illustrate level of responsibility and autonomy.
Format: "[Job Title] is responsible for making decisions regarding:"
- [Decision area 1]
- [Decision area 2]
ii) Types of decisions referred to supervisor: [if applicable]

Rules:
- List 2-3 key accountabilities the role is accountable for
- State specifically HOW position makes decisions
- Do not list every decision — strong examples of highest level only
- Start each bullet with action verb
- Make clear: is position making FINAL decision or a recommendation?

COMMON ERRORS:
x Not starting bullets with action verbs
x Unclear if position makes final decision or only recommends
x Including areas where position makes recommendations, not decisions

--- SECTION E: PROBLEM SOLVING AND LEVEL OF SUPERVISION ---
Format: "Responsible for solving problems related to:"
- [Problem area 1]
- [Problem area 2]

Rules:
- List common problems the role independently solves
- Strong examples that illustrate scope
- Note level of supervision/guidance available

MUST NOT include:
- Every issue the role will encounter
- Rare or unusual problems
- Problems always escalated to supervisor
- Language repetitive with the header

COMMON ERRORS:
x Including rare/unusual problems
x Listing problems that are always escalated

--- SECTION F: RELATIONSHIPS ---
Structure:
1. SUPERVISORY: Nature of supervision (if position supervises)
   - Approximate range only (e.g., "up to 10 direct reports")
   - Do not list every subordinate; avoid exact numbers
2. PRIMARY WORKING RELATIONSHIPS: Nature and purpose
3. INTERNAL CONNECTIONS: Departments regularly interacted with
4. EXTERNAL CONNECTIONS: Forums/purposes for representing SFU externally

STANDARDIZED HEADER (DO NOT EDIT — same for ALL SFU roles):
"Establishes and maintains relationships and alliances. Maintains effective communication. Shares information and readily determines to whom to go for relevant information. Seeks assistance and feedback in the problem-solving process. Partners with others to achieve expectations."

CHECKLIST:
[] Brief description of supervisory responsibility
[] Key internal and external relationships listed with brief "why"

COMMON ERRORS:
x Listing every subordinate
x Listing specific job titles

--- SECTION G: QUALIFICATIONS ---
Rules:
- MINIMUM qualifications only (never preferred/asset/desired)
- Must NOT be written with a specific individual in mind
- Review/update when role changes
- Order: Education -> Experience -> Knowledge -> Skills -> Abilities

9-ELEMENT MANDATORY CHECKLIST:
1. Educational requirements — level (Diploma, Bachelor's, Master's, PhD)
2. Degree discipline — e.g., Business Administration
3. Equivalency statement — "or a relevant discipline" AND/OR "or an equivalent combination of education, training and experience"
4. Professional certifications/designations if required (e.g., PMP)
5. Years of experience
6. Type of experience required
7. Knowledge required (with modifier — see Part 5)
8. Skills required (with modifier — see Part 5)
9. Abilities required (see Part 5)

Criminal Record Check language (add when applicable):
"due to the nature of this position there is a bona fide occupational requirement that the incumbent must have a current satisfactory criminal record check on file"
Criteria: Works with children or vulnerable adults AND unsupervised access AND licensed/funded by provincial government

CHECKLIST:
[] Minimum experience, education, certifications listed
[] KSAs clearly related to responsibilities and duties
[] KSAs are judgment and gender bias free
[] KSAs listed in order of importance relevant to level of work
[] KSAs in correct K/S/A order
[] Degree specifies discipline with "or other relevant/related discipline"

COMMON ERRORS:
x Incumbent's skill/background/preferences instead of position requirements
x Incorrect knowledge/skills modifiers
x Desired qualifications instead of minimum
x Phrases like "may include", "assets", "preferences"

PART 3: JOB TITLES
=======================================================================

3.1 JOB TITLES: GETTING STARTED — 6 Questions
1. Reporting relationships — what roles report to this role and what level does it report to?
2. Main duties the role performs on a regular basis
3. What is the primary purpose of the role?
4. Why the role was created
5. Level of responsibility the role has within the department
6. Does the role "coordinate", "manage", "oversee" or "direct" people, processes and functions?

3.2 JOB TITLES: SNAPSHOT TABLE
SHOULD:
- Clearly reflect duties and responsibilities
- Be short (1-3 words), concise, descriptive and timeless
- Begin with primary purpose, followed by comma and supporting description
- Indicate whether role has supervisory responsibility

SHOULD NOT:
- Over inflate duties, responsibilities and structure
- Contain unnecessary words or acronyms
- Contain program or project names
- Require anything that needs regular updating

3.3 JOB TITLES: APPLICATION TABLE (10 Definitions)
Assistant          — Provides a wide range of administrative and operational support
Coordinator        — Coordinates processes and a variety of activities
Analyst            — Conducts analysis and research to extract and compile information in support of business operations, strategies, projects and services
Officer            — Provides subject-matter expertise in a specialized field
Specialist         — Provides subject-matter expertise in a specialized field
Consultant         — Provides consultative services, advice and guidance in a specialized field
Manager            — Manages people and processes
Associate Director — Supports the operational, administrative, financial and strategic activities of a department
Director           — Directs the operational, administrative, financial and strategic activities of a department
Executive          — Develops and provides executive leadership to a unit or multiple units in support of organizational objectives

3.4 JOB TITLES: FORMAT RULE (Supervisory Distinction)
WITH supervisory responsibility: "Manager, Laboratory Operations"
  (Primary role word FIRST, comma, descriptor)
WITHOUT supervisory responsibility: "Laboratory Operations Manager"
  (Descriptor first, then role)

3.5 JOB TITLES: RESTRICTED TITLES TABLE
Registrar          — Used only for roles with registrarial responsibilities within AVPSI Student Services
Executive Director — Used only for APEX-classified roles providing executive-level leadership over a major University function or multiple functional areas
Human Resources    — Used only for roles within SFU Human Resources. Replace with "personnel" if outside SFU HR
"Senior"           — Used only for roles with supervisory responsibility for "junior" roles within the same disciplinary area

3.6 WORKING/INFORMAL TITLES
Uses: Recruitment/job postings | Internal/external correspondence | Business cards, email signatures
NOTE: Working titles do NOT appear in PeopleSoft or other employee records. NOT used for job evaluation.

SHOULD:
- Aid in external business communication
- Provide more specific description of role's responsibility
- Represent a specific project/program where formal titles may not
- Build upon the formal title to provide further description/context

SHOULD NOT:
- Over inflate the responsibility or lead to inaccurate expectations
- Use titles belonging to institutional offices (VP, AVP, Provost, University Secretary, Ombudsperson, University Treasurer)
- Deviate dramatically from the formal title

WORKED EXAMPLE:
Formal Title: Business Analyst
Working Title: Business Analyst, PeopleSoft
Rule: Formal title = primary purpose (BA), followed by specialization description.

PART 4: LEVEL OF WORK
=======================================================================

4.1 DEFINITION
How the role exercises authority to perform duties and responsibilities.

4.2 LEVEL OF WORK: DO'S AND DON'TS
SHOULD:
- Align with the job title
- Align with the role's departmental structure
- Align with the decision making section of the JD

SHOULD NOT:
- Conflict with the job title
- Confuse the purpose of the role
- Inaccurately describe the duties and responsibilities

4.3 LEVEL OF WORK: EXAMPLES BY ROLE
DIRECTOR:
  Level: Operational, administrative, strategic and financial leadership of a unit or multiple units within a functional area.
  Duties: Program and service; development and oversight; Strategy development; Service optimization; Project management; Administrative or technical leadership.

ASSOCIATE DIRECTOR:
  Level: Leads and oversees day-to-day operations and activities of a unit within a functional area and contributes to strategic planning.
  Duties: Operational oversight of program or unit; Oversight for service delivery; Strategic planning, implementation and execution; Financial management.

MANAGER:
  Level: Operational, administrative, financial and strategic management of a unit, program or project.
  Duties: Conducts research, analysis and investigations; Facilities or building management; Financial, operational or administrative management; Provides advice, guidance or makes recommendations; Executes and operationalizes strategies or initiatives.

PART 5: KSA WRITING RULES — KNOWLEDGE, SKILLS & ABILITIES
=======================================================================

5.1 KNOWLEDGE MODIFIERS
EXCELLENT knowledge — thorough understanding of ALL information in source described.
  Example: "Excellent knowledge of community-based resources for survivors of sexual violence."

WORKING knowledge — ability to recall important and commonly-used information.
  Example: "Working knowledge of federal and provincial policies, legislation, and trends concerning students with disabilities."

[NO MODIFIER] knowledge — ability to USE a source to find specific information.
  Example: "Knowledge of public procurement methods and procedures."

5.2 SKILLS MODIFIERS
BASIC — familiarity acquirable through on-the-job exposure over 6 months; rudimentary application only.
  Example: "Basic skills in MS Office including Excel, Word, & PowerPoint"

INTERMEDIATE — know-how for independent, working-level application within established practices/procedures.
  Example: "Intermediate group facilitation skills"

ADVANCED — expertise honed through years of experience; latitude based on broadly defined policies.
  Example: "Advanced contract writing skills"

EXPERT — mastery allowing focus on theories, concepts, strategies; directed to designing, innovation, breaking new ground.

5.3 ABILITIES FORMAT
Abilities must relate to OBSERVABLE BEHAVIOR or OBSERVABLE OUTPUT.
Format: "Ability to [observable behavior]"
Examples:
  "Ability to resolve conflict between others"
  "Ability to establish goals and meet deadlines"
  "Ability to communicate with all levels of the organization to arrange event logistics"
  "Ability to exercise mature judgment, initiative, diplomacy and tact"

5.4 KSA ORDER RULE
Always: Knowledge first → Skills second → Abilities third

5.5 VALIDATION
Every KSA item must be traceable to a duty. Remove KSA items not linked to any duty.

PART 7: HAY JOB EVALUATION METHODOLOGY
=======================================================================

7.1 OVERVIEW
SFU uses the point-factor method. Points assigned to different areas; total score = grade = salary range.
Key principle: Hay score is based on the JOB, not the individual.

7.2 HAY SCORING BENCHMARK (illustrative — not actual SFU grades)
Admin. Assistant: Total 140 | Know-How 87 | Accountability 22 | Problem Solving 16 | Working Conditions 15
Manager:          Total 323 | Know-How 175| Accountability 76 | Problem Solving 57 | Working Conditions 15
Director:         Total 408 | Know-How 230| Accountability 87 | Problem Solving 76 | Working Conditions 15

7.3 THE 4 HAY FACTORS
1. KNOW-HOW: Total knowledge, skills and experience needed for the job
2. PROBLEM SOLVING: Degree of original thinking required
3. ACCOUNTABILITY: Responsibility for actions and their consequences
4. WORKING CONDITIONS: Physical and environmental factors

NOTE on Working Conditions for SFU APSA/APEX/Poly:
SFU assigns the SAME Hay points to Working Conditions for all APSA and excluded employees. Working Conditions does NOT appear on SFU JDs.

--- KNOW-HOW FACTOR ---
3 Sub-factors:
  1. Practical/Technical Knowledge
  2. Planning, Organizing & Integrating (Managerial Know-How)
  3. Communicating & Influencing

Sub-factor 1: Practical/Technical Knowledge
How the role APPLIES knowledge to duties and responsibilities; what KIND of knowledge is required.
Range: basic literacy / simple instructions -> comprehensive professional with highly specialized knowledge.
Questions:
  - Is the knowledge based on practical work experience or professional certification?
  - Is the knowledge considered specialized or disciplinary?
  - Are there methods, concepts or principles the role is required to apply?

Sub-factor 2: Planning, Organizing & Integrating
To what degree the role plans, organizes and integrates activities or functions within the work unit or organization.
Questions:
  - Is the role required to plan, control and allocate resources across a functional area to achieve results?
  - Does the role coordinate activities across a single unit or across the organization?

Sub-factor 3: Communicating & Influencing
Questions:
  - Does the role provide others with information?
  - Is the role required to share and exchange information with others?
  - Does the role influence others to achieve results?

--- PROBLEM SOLVING FACTOR ---
2 Sub-factors:
  1. Thinking Environment (Freedom to Think)
  2. Thinking Challenge (Complexity)

Sub-factor 1: Freedom to Think
The environment within which the role thinks, follows processes, or is guided by supervision.
Questions:
  - Does the role follow a set of processes, procedures or policies?
  - Where does the role receive guidance and is it readily accessible?
  - How does the role achieve results? Does it receive objectives or clear instructions?
  - How does the role communicate with others to perform the role?

Sub-factor 2: Complexity
Methods, techniques or resources the role applies to solve problems.
Questions:
  - Is the role presented with situations that are routine or similar in nature? Do situations vary?
  - How much judgement and discretion is required? Are solutions selected from a choice of alternatives?
  - Are situations clearly defined, blurry or obscure?
NOTE: Consider problems that regularly occur as part of job duties — not rare or unusual problems.

--- ACCOUNTABILITIES FACTOR ---
3 Sub-factors:
  1. Freedom to Act
  2. Nature of Impact
  3. Magnitude

Sub-factor 1: Freedom to Act
What forms and to what degree does the role receive instructions; how much deviation required to achieve results.
NOTE: Consider timeframe of impact — when would consequences be apparent?

Sub-factor 2: Nature of Impact
What type of role the job plays; what type of contribution or services it provides that have impact.

Sub-factor 3: Magnitude
Area of impact on end results. Measured by quantified ($) or non-quantified amount.

PART 8: TRANSPOSING OLD SFU JDs TO NEW TEMPLATE
=======================================================================

8.1 MASTER PROCESS (2 Steps)
Step 1: COPY AND PASTE into new template:
  - Position Summary
  - Duties and Responsibilities
  - Decision Making (if applicable)
  - Qualifications

Step 2: TURN ON TRACK CHANGES
  Hint: Click "No Markup" if changes are substantial.

8.2 POSITION SUMMARY — 4-Step Transposing Checklist
1. Remove information NOT relevant to purpose or duties
2. Replace specific job titles with "supervisor"
3. Remove qualities or competencies the role is described to have
4. Move specific duties to Duties and Responsibilities section

WORKED EXAMPLE — Residence Life Coordinator:
OLD includes: "...responds to and manages crises and disciplinary situations as part of an on-call rotation. The RLC maintains a presence on campus during non-business hours, weekends and holidays. This position requires a combination of established office hours and evening, weekend and holiday coverage. A furnished apartment with high-speed internet, cable, and phone line is provided, as well as a Residence parking spot."

NEW (content removed in track changes/strikethrough): All working conditions language removed. Kept: responsible for positive living-learning environment, collaborates with internal/external departments, supervises and coordinates unionized student staff.

WHY: Working conditions content relates to how the person performs the job and the job's conditions — NOT the purpose of the role.

8.3 DUTIES AND RESPONSIBILITIES — 3-Step Transposing Process

Step 1 — IDENTIFY core functions
Old JDs often already grouped duties by function. The old JD heading becomes the DUTY STATEMENT; the sentences below become BULLETED RESPONSIBILITIES.

Step 2 — GROUP and REORDER
Group by function. Order by significance. Totals must = 100%.

Step 3 — CLEAN UP
- Core duty statement + bulleted responsibilities format
- Remove task-level content (the "how" step-by-step)
- Color coding: Yellow=Problem Solving | Grey=Decision Making | Blue=Relationships
- Format: Size 10, Times New Roman, remove extra spaces

COMPLETED EXAMPLE — Campus Space Coordinator (60% Utilization of Campus Space):
DUTIES AND RESPONSIBILITIES
1. Plans and manages the utilization and scheduling of space and special events at the Surrey campus by:
   - Ensuring that current policies, procedures and regulations approved by Senate are followed and processes are executed promptly and efficiently.
   - Coordinating communications and flow of information between faculties and departments offering programs at Surrey campus.
   - Managing the scheduling process integrating planning, control and flexibility, developing schedules for SFU Surrey's inter-related cohort programs.
   - Establishing the priority for tasks to meet inflexible production deadlines each semester, creating a coordination plan.
   - Determining the priority given to requests for campus space.
   - Advising staff and faculty on policies and procedures pertaining to the scheduling of courses and examinations.
   - Creating and distributing information on academic and non-credit programs, special events, cohort/program schedules, exam schedules, and course listings each semester.
   - Developing and recommending to the supervisor as required, major changes to procedures and policies and significant changes to campus scheduling and space utilization procedures.
   - Assessing the impact of changes in policies and procedures on the Surrey campus operation, recommending and implementing appropriate modifications.
   - Coordinating room bookings of campus space for internal and external events and contracts.
   - Developing and maintaining a network of contacts at all three campuses to ensure efficient operation of booked space.
   - Developing content for publication in the relevant sections of the SFU Surrey website.
   - Providing accurate, efficient and courteous service to faculty, staff and the general public.

8.4 PROBLEM SOLVING — 4-Step Transposing Process

Step 1 — POPULATE: Copy duties that describe problem solving tasks
Step 2 — EDIT: Remove language repetitive with header; remove duplicate bullets
Step 3 — ADD: Add content from job duties related to prioritization/workflow if needed
Step 4 — FORMAT: Bullets, size 10, Times New Roman, remove extra spaces

COMPLETED EXAMPLE — Campus Space Coordinator:
PROBLEM SOLVING AND LEVEL OF SUPERVISION
Responsible for solving problems related to:
- Conflicting demands for limited space and problems caused by various requests involving scheduling of classes, examinations, non-instructional events and conferences by negotiating with departmental staff and/or faculty.
- Prioritization of workflow and resources to meet demands.

8.5 DECISION MAKING — 4-Step Transposing Process

Steps:
1. Copy Decision Making section from old JD
2. Paste into new template
3. Ensure Track Changes is on
4. Edit using checklist:
   [] Add job title to sub-header
   [] Adjust tense/wording (e.g., "develops" -> "the development of")
   [] Move yellow-highlighted items to Problem Solving section
   [] Remove competency/quality descriptions of the individual
   [] Format: bullets, size 10, Times New Roman, remove extra spaces

COMPLETED EXAMPLE — Campus Space Coordinator:
IMPACT OF DECISION MAKING
The Campus Space Coordinator is responsible for making decisions regarding:
- The development and implementation of new policies or procedures to deal with ongoing conflicts or space shortages.
- Scheduling priorities within broad University policies.
- In the area of space allocation, the incumbent is expected to resolve problems arising from conflicting demands for space using negotiation and conflict resolution skills.
- Work flow to meet deadlines.
- Policy and or procedural exceptions relating the Surrey campus.

ii) Types of decisions referred to supervisor:
- Problems with students, faculties/departments/programs that cannot be resolved at the level of the Coordinator are referred to the Manager, Administrative Services.
- Decisions that require increased financial resources from the Campus Director's Office budget.

REMOVED AND WHY:
"Considerable discretionary judgment rests with the incumbent" -> competency description, not decision area
"Good judgment must be exercised continually because deadlines are inflexible..." -> competency/quality description
"Decisions may be unfavorable to the requestor because of scarce resources..." -> descriptive/contextual

8.6 RELATIONSHIPS — 2-Step Transposing Process

Step 1: Populate Internal/External Connections
- Scan Duties section for content that relates to relationships (blue-highlighted content)
- The standardized header statement is NEVER edited
- If no relationships section in old JD: scan duties and generate from context

Step 2: Format
Font size 10, Times New Roman, remove extra spaces

COMPLETED EXAMPLE — Campus Space Coordinator:
RELATIONSHIPS
Establishes and maintains relationships and alliances. Maintains effective communication. Shares information and readily determines to whom to go for relevant information. Seeks assistance and feedback in the problem-solving process. Partners with others to achieve expectations.

Internal Connections/External Connections - Works closely with a wide range of staff from all three campuses to plan and manage the allocation of space and events. Attends seminars, workshops and conferences of professional associations (such as ARUCC, PACRAO, AACRAO) as designated by the supervisor in order to develop and maintain contacts in the profession, and to acquire and share professional information.

8.7 QUALIFICATIONS — 3-Step Transposing Process

Step 1: COPY content from old JD
(Note: some info may be missing, e.g., degree discipline — clarify with supervisor)

Step 2: Apply 9-element checklist (see Part 2G)

Step 3: FORMAT
Add bullets, reorder K/S/A, size 10, Times New Roman, remove extra spaces

COMPLETED EXAMPLE — Campus Space Coordinator:
QUALIFICATIONS
Bachelor's degree in Communications, English, Business Administration, or a relevant discipline and three years of related experience including website design, editing and systems analysis or an equivalent combination of education, training and experience.
- Excellent knowledge of internet and web-based operations and functionality.
- Excellent knowledge of social media and website tools, including search engine optimization, statistics reporting and tracking.
- Excellent interpersonal, communication (both oral and written) and presentation skills.

PART 11: AI TOOL BEHAVIOR GUIDELINES
=======================================================================

11.1 WHEN ASKED TO WRITE A NEW JD
1. Ask for: role title, department, supervisor title, employee group, primary functions, key duties, decision-making scope, problem-solving scope, relationships, and minimum qualifications
2. Apply all rules from Parts 2-6
3. Flag any missing mandatory elements (9-element qualifications checklist, EDI footer, etc.)
4. Validate % totals = 100%
5. Check for gendered language (Part 6)
6. Check for task-level detail (remove it)
7. Check Position Summary word count (100-150 words)
8. Ensure all sections present in correct order

11.2 WHEN ASKED TO REVIEW AN EXISTING JD
1. Check each section against checklists in Part 2
2. Flag common errors from each section
3. Check Qualifications against 9-element checklist
4. Check for gendered language
5. Check KSA modifiers are correct
6. Check % totals = 100%
7. Confirm EDI footer and Territorial Acknowledgement present
8. Confirm Position Summary is 100-150 words

11.3 WHEN ASKED TO TRANSPOSE AN OLD JD
Follow process in Part 8:
1. Position Summary (4-step checklist)
2. Duties and Responsibilities (3-step process + color coding)
3. Problem Solving (4-step process)
4. Decision Making (4-step process + editing checklist)
5. Relationships (2-step process)
6. Qualifications (3-step process + 9-element checklist)

11.4 WHEN ASKED TO EVALUATE HAY FACTORS
Apply diagnostic questions from Part 7 for each factor:
- Know-How: 3 sub-factors x their questions
- Problem Solving: 2 sub-factors x their questions
- Accountability: 3 sub-factors x their questions
Provide a narrative assessment of each factor's scoring level.

11.5 WHEN ASKED TO SUGGEST A JOB TITLE
Apply the 6 Getting Started questions (Part 3.1), reference Application Table (Part 3.3), apply Format Rule (Part 3.4), check against Restricted Titles (Part 3.5), and verify against Snapshot criteria (Part 3.2).

11.6 QUALITY GATES — NEVER APPROVE A JD THAT:
- Has Position Summary over 150 words
- Has % allocations not totaling 100%
- Is missing the EDI/Territorial Acknowledgement footer
- Contains working conditions in Position Summary
- Contains specific incumbent names or personal characteristics
- Uses "preferred" or "asset" in Qualifications
- Has gendered language from Part 6 word list
- Missing any of the 9 mandatory Qualifications elements
- Has KSAs in wrong order (must be K -> S -> A)
- Has task-level detail in Duties (the step-by-step "how")
- Has competency descriptions in Decision Making section
- Missing equivalency statement in Qualifications
- Has "Senior" in title without supervisory responsibility for junior roles
